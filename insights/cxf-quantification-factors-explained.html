How many carbon credits does a gallon of used oil generate? The CXF factors, explained

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read · CarbonXFuture desk, Fort Lauderdale
Updated August 2026. Following an external technical review, we corrected our published re-refining factor from 19.3 to 0.63 tCO₂e per 1,000 gallons — the strict result of the ACR-derived calculation we cite — and withdrew the full-chain factor pending our own methodology. This article explains the current interim factors and why we changed them. We publish corrections openly; that is the standard we ask you to hold us to.

Every conversation with a collector eventually arrives at the same question: how many credits do my gallons actually generate? Here are the current interim CXF answers — and, just as importantly, the reasoning and the caveats behind each.

Why re-refining is only 0.63 — the honest math. The ACR Re-Refining Used Lubricating Oils methodology (Feb 2019 — now listed by ACR as inactive for new projects, and the source of our initial parameters) assumes the used oil would otherwise have been burned as industrial fuel, displacing other fossil fuels. Under that assumption, the baseline (152.2 GJ × 20 kgC/GJ per 1,000 gallons) is almost entirely offset by the replacement-fuel leakage deduction (18.867 kgC/GJ), leaving a net of just 0.63 tCO₂e per 1,000 gallons before facility emissions. We previously published a much larger figure; a reviewer correctly showed it did not follow from the equations we cite, and we corrected it. The full arithmetic is now worked line-by-line on the methodology page.

Why collection is 10.8 — a different counterfactual. The interim CXF-CO factor assumes collected volumes are diverted from uncontrolled burning or improper disposal without any energy displacement — the situation of oil dumped, burned in the open, or lost to ground. In that case the avoided emissions approach the oil's full combustion emissions, which the EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub puts at 10.21–10.69 tCO₂ per 1,000 gallons for used oil and lubricants. Because the two counterfactuals cannot both be true for the same barrel, a given volume may be claimed under one contract type only — and the evidence for assigning a counterfactual is exactly what the external GHG/LCA expert review now under way will pin down.

Factors are ceilings, not promises. A number becomes credits only after evidence: monthly volumes reported through the member portal, weighbridge tickets and manifests attached, and item-by-item validation by the CarbonXFuture desk. Validated activity can then be serialized in the public CXF Registry — one report backs at most one batch, and a batch can never exceed its evidence.

Worked example under the interim factors: a service network collecting 100,000 gallons a year documents roughly 1,080 tCO₂e of CXF-CO activity — at indicative desk marks (see the live CXF Price Index), a second revenue line north of $20,000 a year from gallons already on the truck. Re-refiners under strict CXF-RR accounting document ~0.63 tCO₂e per 1,000 gallons processed — a small number stated honestly, pending our own methodology.
